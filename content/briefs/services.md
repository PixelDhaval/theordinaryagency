## Content Brief: design services

### Search Intent
Commercial. Searchers are comparing what a studio offers before deciding whether to look closer at a specific service. Audience: same as homepage — founders/marketers scoping a project who aren't yet sure which specific service they need, or who want to confirm the agency covers everything their brand requires. Google rewards a hub/category-page format: short intro, clear categorized list linking to deeper service pages, not a single long article.

### Content Gaps and Opportunities
- **Topic gap:** Competitors rarely group this exact mix (branding + packaging + pitch decks + social + brochures + UI/UX) on one hub — most hubs are either print-focused or digital-product-focused, not both. This page can own that combined positioning.
- **Depth gap:** Hub pages in this space tend to be link lists with little explanation of *when* a client needs which service. Adding a short "which service do I need" framing (e.g., "launching a product → branding + packaging", "raising funding → pitch deck") adds real decision-making value competitors skip.
- **Quality gap:** Service names on competitor hubs are often vague ("Brand & Identity") rather than matching how a client would actually search ("packaging design", "pitch deck design"). Using exact, specific service names as the card titles and H2s improves both clarity and SEO match.

### Site Structure Coverage Rule
This is a hub page — every one of the six live service pages must appear as its own section with an internal link: Branding, Packaging Design, Pitch Deck Design, Social Media Design, Brochure Design, UI/UX Design. No sub-service should be invented or omitted.

### Winning Outline

**H1:** Design Services
**URL Slug:** /services
**Target Word Count:** ~900-1,100 words (competitor avg ~2,900; shorter because depth belongs on the six child pages, not here)

- **Intro (60-80 words):** One paragraph framing the studio's range and primary keyword "design services" placed in the first sentence.
- **"Which service do you need" framing (100-120 words):** Short scenario-based guide (launching a brand → branding; shipping a product → packaging; raising funds → pitch deck; growing an audience → social media design; printed collateral → brochure design; digital product → UI/UX) — this is the differentiation section competitors skip.
- **Six service sections, one per discipline (each ~100-130 words, 6 total ≈ 650 words):**
  1. **Branding** (H2, secondary keyword "brand identity design") — 2-3 sentence description, 2-3 bullet deliverables, link to /services/branding
  2. **Packaging Design** (H2) — same structure, link to /services/packaging-design
  3. **Pitch Deck Design** (H2) — link to /services/pitch-deck-design
  4. **Social Media Design** (H2) — link to /services/social-media-design
  5. **Brochure Design** (H2) — link to /services/brochure-design
  6. **UI/UX Design** (H2) — link to /services/ui-ux-design
- **Closing CTA (40-50 words):** Link to /contact for scoping a project.

### Unique Angle and Information Gain
The "which service do you need" framing is the real information gain: it answers a question (how do these six services map to a client's actual situation) that competitor hub pages leave unanswered, turning a plain link list into a decision-making tool.
